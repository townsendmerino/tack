### Why each void arm is void

- **qwen2.5-7b, CPU.** All six TTFT requests at 15,360 tokens were refused with a 413: the request needs about
  4.04 GB on goinfer's CPU path. Memory pressure reached *warning* during the arm. Even its measured cell at 8192
  is 62 times the incumbent's.
- **gemma-4-E4B and E2B.** night.py's timeouts (80 and 55 minutes) stopped both inside the 15,360 cells, before
  decode. Cold prefill on this goinfer build ran at about 18–41 tok/s, against 915 for the 1.5B. That is goinfer's
  defect P28, not a property of the models. Both arms are re-run once goinfer reports the fix (amendment 4).
- **phi3.** goinfer declined Metal for its quantization (`ActQuantGroup 32`) and served it on the CPU (amendment 4).
  The arm is retired (amendment 5). Its four failed `check` rows were taken on the CPU and are observations only:
  three 400s for a missing tool-calling template, and a long prompt that produced no tokens.

## The Mac's default

**No measured Mac arm passes, so the model stays on nobara.** The only measured eligible arm is the 7B on Metal. Its
reused TTFT at 8192 is **3.84 times** the incumbent's, against a bar of 2.0. That is far outside the 10% parked band.
Its decode, 29.9 tok/s, clears the 10 tok/s bar.

**This is not final for the Mac.** The two Gemma 4 arms are void, E2B with an eligible grade and E4B parked. They are
re-run after goinfer's P28 fix, and that run decides whether a Mac arm can replace the incumbent. Their figures above
come from the P28 build and are not graded.

**The floor held.** The 0.5B, the model least likely to pass, failed smoke at 2 of 20, so the smoke set is not too
easy (amendment 2).

The planned window is not set for the Mac, since the default stays on nobara.

## For the app: the 7B on the Mac alone (DESIGN R12)

The 2.0x bar decides where the owner's model runs, because he also has nobara. The bundled app's users have only the
Mac. So these are the absolute figures for Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct `q4_k_m` on Metal, on the M1 Pro with 16 GB. They are
evidence for risk R12, separate from the grade:

- **It is agent-capable here.** It got the eligible grade: `check` all rows passing, and smoke 17 of 20 on both runs
  with 0 malformed.
- **Reused-prefix TTFT** (the next turn of a conversation) is about **3.1 s at 8k tokens** and 4.1 s at 15k. The
  medians are 3,091 ms and 4,136 ms.
- **Cold TTFT** is 19.8 s at 4k, 43 s at 8k and 89 s at 15k. That is a new conversation or a lost cache.
- **Decode is 29.9 tok/s.**
- **A 16k window fits.** goinfer admitted `-ctx 16384` on `metal-resident (int4)`: 4.4 GB of weights and 0.9 GB of
  KV, with 2.9 GB left for a request's own prefill at the night run and 3.4 GB at the day fit check on goinfer main.
  That headroom is the memory free when the model loads, so it shrinks with other apps open. The 0.2 GB in the
  earlier day fit check came from the superseded build `41e13b26`, which planned 1.8 GB of KV for the same window.
- **Memory pressure stayed *normal*** through the arm.

R12 says no agent-capable model fits beside the owner's other apps. A 7B does fit and pass on a 16 GB Mac, at about 3
seconds a turn once its prefix is cached. What remains is how it behaves with other apps open, which this run did not
load the machine with.

## Observations, not results

- **Qwen2.5-Coder makes no tool calls.** The 1.5B and 0.5B coder models answered in text on 18 and 15 of the 20 smoke
  tasks. Their 2 passes are the two tasks that call no tool. The 0.5B's failed `check` row is the livelock shape: it
  asked for the tool again instead of answering. This is the same tokenizer and template family as the eligible 7B
  Instruct.
- **E2B's smoke misses.** Two of E2B's misses called `glob` where `grep` was wanted. The third used a pattern without
  `_test.go`. E4B's failed `check` row is stop sequences: the reply never reached the stop sequence.
- **RSS says nothing about Metal footprint.** The Metal arms report 0.3–5.7 GB of RSS, because weights mapped into
  unified memory are not counted. The registration's footprint figure is not graded here.
